# Digital Estates

*Rendered Rants. A vlog: JAMES films himself on his phone, hand-held on the street,
propped up at home and on the dash in the estate car. All lines are James's (his
own routine, verbatim apart from spellings for the voice: numbers and "2FA" as
words, "Mr." as "Mister").*

**Title card:** Digital Estates

---

**THE STREET, DAY.** A terraced street. JAMES walks towards us, filming himself on his phone at arm's length.

JAMES (relaxed, warm): When my granddad died, splitting up his estate was basically a CrossFit workout.
JAMES (flat, glancing left): Four hundred vinyl records.
JAMES (deadpan, a nod): Heavy as lead.
JAMES (weary): Taking three trips in the estate car just to get them to my house.

**THE STREET.** Closer on his face as he walks.

JAMES (brightening, finger up): But you knew exactly where you stood!
JAMES (rhythm of three, hitting "boom"): He bought them, he owned them, boom, they were ours.

**THE STREET.** Still walking.

JAMES (conspiratorial, glancing right): Contrast that with my mate Tom.
JAMES (softer): Tom's dad died a few months ago.
JAMES (sincere): And look, Tom's dad was a legend, we miss him, this is not about grief.
JAMES (relishing each adjective): This is about the absolute, farcical, administrative nightmare of modern life.
JAMES (rueful, pointing): One of those days where the universe just points at you and laughs.

**JAMES'S LOUNGE.** Phone propped up. James on the sofa, in front of shelves of vinyl and a turntable.

JAMES (proud): See, Tom's dad was a proper first-generation digital native.
JAMES: Had a massive Steam library.
JAMES (scandalised): Thousands of pounds worth of games.
JAMES (brisk): Tom's the executor, wants to do the right thing, decides to pass the games down to his teenage nephew.
JAMES: So Tom emails the platform.
JAMES (as Tom, cheerful, shrugging): *Here's the death certificate, lads. Let's transfer the account.*

**LOUNGE.** Right up close.

JAMES (leaning in): What do they do?
JAMES (sharp): *Bam.*
JAMES: Gone.
JAMES (appalled, head shake): Vaporized.

**LOUNGE.** Back a bit.

JAMES (robotic agent, hands on hips): "Licenses expire upon death, sir."
JAMES (same voice): Account permanently closed.
JAMES (incredulous): In trying to be a good guy and do the right thing, Tom legally incinerated his nephew's inheritance in about four seconds.
JAMES (dry, facepalm): Well done, Tom.

**THE ESTATE CAR, PARKED.** Phone on the dash mount. Crates of records in the boot behind him.

JAMES: Slightly bruised, he moves on to the financial stuff.
JAMES (knowing look): Dad was into crypto.
JAMES: Left behind a hardware wallet with a decent chunk of change on it.
JAMES (warning): But Tom needs the 24-word seed phrase.
JAMES: Dad kept a backup in a secure email.
JAMES (finger up): Smart!
JAMES (tense): So Tom sits at the PC, types in the password, hits enter.

**CAR.** Close.

JAMES (hushed): Screen blinks.
JAMES (automated voice, worried brows): *"Please enter the 6-digit code sent to your mobile device."*

**CAR.**

JAMES (worried): You can physically see the cold sweat form on Tom's neck.
JAMES (sarcastic): Because Tom is a *highly efficient* executor.
JAMES: His very first act, yesterday morning, was calling the telecom provider to cancel his dad's phone contract.
JAMES (clipped, head shake): SIM card? Dead.
JAMES: Number? Deactivated.
JAMES (loud, shrug): That Two-Factor authentication chain? Smashed to pieces.

**CAR.** Close.

JAMES (low, worried): He's not just locked out of the crypto.
JAMES: He's locked out of the cloud.
JAMES (mock-epic): A decade of family photos, videos, memories, suddenly sealed behind an impenetrable cryptographic blast door.

**THE HIGH STREET.** Selfie walk past the shops: a phone unlocking shop, a café, the solicitors Pemberton & Gall, a record shop.

JAMES: Panicking, Tom goes to the family solicitor.
JAMES (glancing left): You know the type.
JAMES (fond, nod): Lovely old boy.
JAMES: Still dictates onto mini-cassettes.
JAMES: Smells like mahogany and dust.

**HIGH STREET.** Still walking.

JAMES (frantic, as Tom): Tom sits there raving about cryptographic lock-outs, severed 2FA loops, evaporated software licenses...
JAMES (slowing, a nod): and the solicitor just nods, adjusts his glasses, and goes,
JAMES (plummy old solicitor, finger up): "Right then. I shall write a stern letter to Mr. Apple."

**HIGH STREET.** Close on his face.

JAMES (laughing a little): It's hilarious.
JAMES (slowing): But then you stop laughing, and you realize...
JAMES (blunt, worried): we are so screwed.

**THE STREET.** Walking home, ranting at the phone.

JAMES (ramping up): For years, MI5 and the police have been crying about this!
JAMES (mock-ominous): End-to-end encryption.
JAMES: Unbreakable codes.
JAMES (arms crossed): Tech companies refusing backdoors.
JAMES (sharp turn): But now?
JAMES: That military-grade encryption has finally come home to roost.
JAMES: It's no longer just stopping terrorists.
JAMES (exasperated, shrug): It's stopping grieving families from getting the photos from last year's Christmas dinner!

**LOUNGE.** Back on the sofa.

JAMES (thoughtful): We spent twenty years letting tech companies terrify us into building these unassailable digital fortresses to keep out the hackers.
JAMES (sincere): But we forgot to build a front door for the people we leave behind.

**LOUNGE.** Close.

JAMES (slow): We didn't build digital homes.
JAMES (quiet): We built digital tombs.

**LOUNGE.**

JAMES: So here we are, rushing to digitize our entire existence.
JAMES (incredulous): Are we seriously prepared to be the first generation in human history that leaves absolutely nothing behind,
JAMES (exasperated, shrug): just because our grieving kids couldn't pass a CAPTCHA proving they know what a crosswalk looks like?

**THE STREET.** Walking.

JAMES (decisive): Honestly, if I get hit by a bus tomorrow, forget the digital lawyers.
JAMES (triumphant, finger up): I'm having my 24-word crypto seed phrase laser-engraved, one letter at a time, across all 400 of my granddad's vinyl records!

**THE ESTATE CAR.** The crates of vinyl behind him.

JAMES (smug): If my family wants my digital legacy, they're going to have to earn it the old-fashioned way—
JAMES (delighted): three trips in the estate car and a double hernia.

*He nods, pleased with himself, and waves goodbye to the phone.* End card.
