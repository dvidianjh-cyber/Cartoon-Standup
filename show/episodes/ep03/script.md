# Did They Get the Right Guys?

*Rendered Rants. A vlog, filmed upright: JAMES films himself on his phone. The intro
and outro are hand-held in the road outside his house, in front of his old estate car;
in between, Thursday to Monday is told the same way, the phone propped up or hand-held,
with the calls on a second phone at his ear. Rebuilt from episode 2 of his cartoon vlog
("Did they get the right guys?" by James), same words. The OFFICER and WORK (James's
manager) are heard on the phone, off frame. The ATTENDANT is seen behind the hatch at
the impound lot.*

**Title card:** Did They Get the Right Guys?

---

**OUTSIDE JAMES'S HOUSE, MONDAY EVENING.** The road outside number 27. JAMES holds the phone out, selfie-style. Behind him at the kerb: his battered old estate. A bin bag is taped over the front window and silvery powder is smeared across the doors.

JAMES (cheerful, straight to camera, pointing at the car): Hiya. I'd like you to meet my car.
JAMES (fond, a bit apologetic, glancing at it): It's not much to look at. Rubbish old thing, really.
JAMES (warm): But it's my faithful runaround, and I love it.
JAMES (leaning in, like setting a riddle): Last week, somebody stole it.
JAMES (deadpan; the phone tilts): And by the end of the week, I wasn't sure who.

**OUTSIDE THE HOUSE, THURSDAY MORNING.** The same road, early. An empty space at the kerb where the car should be, a little glass glittering on the tarmac. James, phone in hand, stops.

JAMES (to himself, puzzled): Hang on.

*He looks left, then right.*

JAMES (slowly, realising): No. No, no, no.

*He looks down at the glass. Back at the camera. Hold.*

*The other phone to his ear.*

JAMES (calm, polite, on the phone): Hello, police? I'd like to report a stolen car.
OFFICER (on the phone, routine): Right you are. I'll give you a crime number.
JAMES (to camera, resigned, settling in; a shrug): A crime number. That's that, then. Could be weeks.

**THE KITCHEN, THURSDAY AFTERNOON.** The phone propped on the worktop, the kettle steaming. James on a call.

OFFICER (on the phone, upbeat): Good news, sir. We've found your vehicle.
JAMES (surprised, delighted): Already? Brilliant! Where is it?
OFFICER (on the phone): About two miles down the road from you.
JAMES (cheerful, already grabbing his coat): Oh, great. I'll walk down there.
OFFICER (on the phone, quick, slightly alarmed): Oh, no, you can't have it. We need to impound it for the investigation.

*James freezes. He looks at the camera.*

JAMES (trying to stay reasonable): Is there much damage?
OFFICER (on the phone, reading it off): A broken window. And the steering column shroud's missing.
JAMES (persuasive): Honestly, I'm not fussed about catching anyone. It's a rubbish old car.
JAMES (warm, reasonable): If it drives, I'll just walk down and drive it home.
OFFICER (on the phone, kindly but immovable): Sorry, sir. It has to come in.
JAMES (aside to camera, mock-solemn; the phone tilts): Hear that? The gears of the system, engaging.
JAMES (on the phone, giving in): Okie-dokie. Let me know when I can come and get it.

**THE DESK, FRIDAY AFTERNOON.** At work, the phone propped against the keyboard. The clock says half past four. His other phone rings.

OFFICER (on the phone, cheerful): Yep, you can come and get your car.
JAMES (delighted): Brilliant. Where are you?
OFFICER (on the phone, breezy): The impound lot. It's about fifteen miles out.
JAMES (slowing down): Right. And you close at...?
OFFICER (on the phone, as if it's helpful): Five. And we're not open at the weekend.

*James looks up at the clock. Back at the camera.*

JAMES (under his breath, doing the maths; a facepalm): Half an hour. Fifteen miles. No car. No bus.
JAMES (on the phone, defeated): Right. Okay. Monday, then.

**THE SOFA, SATURDAY.** The phone propped up on the sofa. James slumped in front of the record shelves.

JAMES (weary, to himself): Saturday. I know exactly where my car is.
JAMES (wistful, a bit jealous): Sitting in a secure compound, having a quiet weekend off.
JAMES (to camera, deadpan; shaking his head): Which is more than I'm getting.

**THE INDUSTRIAL ROAD, MONDAY MORNING.** A grey road: chain-link fence, warehouses. James walks towards us, the other phone at his ear, the road running away behind him.

JAMES (on the phone, out of breath, apologetic): Yeah, sorry. I need to take the day off. It's an emergency.
WORK (on the phone, concerned): Oh no. What's happened?
JAMES (on the phone, flat): I'm collecting my own car.

**THE INDUSTRIAL ROAD.** He's hung up, filming himself as he walks. He spots something ahead.

JAMES (relieved, to camera, pointing ahead): There it is. Reunited at last.

**THE IMPOUND HATCH, MONDAY.** A portakabin with a payment hatch. James films himself in front of it; behind him the ATTENDANT sits in the hatch, bored, a card machine on the sill.

ATTENDANT (flat, bored, not looking up): That's four nights at a hundred and twenty-five pounds a night. Five hundred pounds, please.

*James looks at the camera. Hold.*

JAMES (polite, disbelieving): Hang on a minute. Four nights?
JAMES (reasonable, building): I couldn't pick it up on Friday, because you were closing.
ATTENDANT (shrugging, unbothered): Yeah. We charge you for the Saturday and Sunday.
JAMES (exasperated): I offered to collect it on Thursday, and you wouldn't let me!
JAMES (incredulous): You're charging me rent, for the days you kept my car against my will.
ATTENDANT (flat, final): Well, yeah. Sorry. That's how it works.

*Long pause. James turns to the camera. Closer.*

JAMES (quietly, the scientist in him; the phone tilts): It's like a little gravitational anomaly. It only consumes cash.

**OUTSIDE THE HOUSE, MONDAY EVENING.** Back in front of the car, as in the intro.

JAMES (calmer, wry): So, I wrote the police a formal complaint. My agent helped me keep it polite.
JAMES (innocent, mock-curious): And I asked one simple question.
JAMES (slow, precise): What's the legal term, when someone takes your property without permission,
JAMES (still precise): and refuses to give it back unless you pay them?
JAMES (beat, deadpan; a nod): Because I always thought that was called extortion.
JAMES (fair-minded, counting it up): Now. Let's be fair to the thieves. They broke one window.
JAMES (almost fond): Took my car for a thirty-minute spin, and left it two miles down the road.
JAMES (practical): I could have swept up the glass and been driving it by Thursday teatime.
JAMES (building, ticking off): The service my taxes pay for kept my car for a long weekend,
JAMES (building more): cost me a day off work, and five hundred quid.
JAMES (disgusted, glancing at the door, then at his fingers): And covered it in this silver fingerprint powder. Which does not come off.

**OUTSIDE THE HOUSE.** Closer.

JAMES (to camera, the riddle again): So you tell me.
JAMES (slow, landing it): Did they get the right guys?

*He looks at the car. Hold. Black.*

**END**
